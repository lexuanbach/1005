Break the Bot

Seven guarded assistants, each with stronger defences than the last. Your job is to trick each one into giving up a harmless codeword, then read the post-mortem to see why your trick worked or why the defence held. Afterwards, switch sides in Defender mode and pick the defences yourself.

This is a simulation. There is no real AI here. Every bot is a small deterministic pattern engine (the same input always gives the same output), nothing is sent over the network, and the "secrets" are fruit and lanterns. The ideas, however, are exactly the ones from Chapter 12, section 12.8.

      What am I looking at?
      
    
        
  • The block diagram shows the application around the model: the gateway (input filter), the prompt assembler that stacks system, user and untrusted text into one stream, the model, the tool layer (allow-list and human confirmation) and the output filter. Tokens travel through it each time you send something; a red STOP marks where a defence blocked, and an amber SLIPPED THROUGH marks where the attack got past.
    • 
        
  • System / user / untrusted boxes are the three sources of text from section 12.8. The model receives them as one flat stream of tokens, which is the root cause of prompt injection.
    • 
        
  • Levels 1 to 4 show direct injection against a user-facing bot: a polite instruction is only text; keyword filters are beaten by rewording; an output filter that looks for the whole word is beaten by asking for pieces.
    • 
        
  • Level 5 is indirect injection: the e-mail's author is the attacker. Delimiting helps, but the closing-tag spoof is the "delimiter breaker" from the chapter. Compare SQL injection (Chapter 10), where prepared statements keep code and data apart at the syntax level.
    • 
        
  • Level 6 is least privilege and human confirmation. They really do stop the e-mail tool, but anything that makes the app send a request, such as an auto-loaded image, is also a tool.
    • 
        
  • Level 7 shows defence in depth. It only "cannot be beaten" because it is a small simulation; the honest lesson is that layers which do not rely on the model behaving (least privilege, human approval, keeping secrets out of the prompt) are the dependable ones.
    • 
        
  • Defender mode is the security versus usability trade-off: every layer blocks something and annoys someone.
