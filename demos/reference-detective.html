Real or Hallucinated?

Your AI assistant just wrote a bibliography for your essay. Some references are real, some are real but quietly wrong (wrong year, wrong journal, a DOI that belongs to another paper), and some never existed. Judge each one, spend time on evidence checks only when you need them, and keep your essay's credibility alive.

How to play: read the reference, open checks with keys 1-4 (each costs 5 points and 15 seconds), then answer Fake, Wrong details or Real (or swipe the card). A verdict backed by the right evidence scores full points; a lucky guess scores less. The second tab trains reference formats (APA, MLA, IEEE); the third is a big-text mode for the lecturer: the class votes by raising hands.

Concept: Chapter 13 - Citation & Responsible AI. The textbook's rule: AI may support your learning, but it does not replace your responsibility to verify sources.

What am I looking at?
On screenLecture concept
The reference card (APA, IEEE or MLA)A reference is a promise that a work exists as described. The format is produced by the same engine as the Citation Workshop in Chapter 13, so a fake looks exactly as tidy as a real one.
The four evidence checksVerification: DOI lookup, author list, journal/volume/issue, year/pages. Real references pass all four; wrong-details references fail exactly one or two; fabricated ones fail the lookup.
"Real, wrong details"The subtle AI failure: the work is real but the reference was reconstructed from memory (wrong year, wrong venue, swapped authors, a DOI that points elsewhere).
The ecosystem diagramThe blocks around a reference: the AI assistant that writes it, your essay (in-text citation), the reference list entry, doi.org and the publisher page, the Crossref metadata registry, the journal record and the author list. Each card is "written" by the AI block; each check sends a lookup token to the block that holds the evidence and back with a match, a mismatch or "not found". After your verdict the origin is traced: a real reference leads to a registry record; a fabricated one has no record, and the AI block lights up. Every block has its own colour (see the legend): a lookup token leaves in the colour of the block that sends it and returns in the colour of the block that answered, and the part of the reference card that a check looked at is underlined in that answering block's colour. The result badges never change colour: green check, red cross, grey empty set. Speed, Pause and Step control the tokens.
Essay credibility meterConsequences: one fabricated source in a submitted essay costs more than a few false alarms (accepting a fake: -20; accepting a wrong-details one: -12; rejecting a good one: -8).
Report cardNames the red flags you missed: generic title, round page numbers, DOI that does not resolve, volume that does not exist, and so on.
Where the data comes fromThe real works were checked in Crossref on 2026-10-18 and each reveal links to its DOI. The fake references are invented for this game (invented names; their titles do not exist in Crossref and their DOIs answer "not found").
